Every dot is an equational law, or a class of laws that imply each other. Small multiplication tables are sent out as probes. A table that obeys law A and breaks law B proves, once and for all, that A does not imply B. What no probe and no prover settles is the residue, marked in amber.

How to read it

Positions come from each law's satisfaction rate on every kind of 3-element table, flattened to two dimensions. Laws that behave alike on small tables sit close together. Blue dots are the 1,415 classes of the Equational Theories Project's finished map, sized by how many laws they hold; faint lines are its cover relations. Teal dots are classes that appear only with five multiplications: each is certified different from every older law by a probe. Violet marks the class of x = y, where laws with only one-element models collapse. It implies every law, and every law implies x = x, so lines to those two are left out; both are pinned to the edge of the chart.

Built on the Equational Theories Project's data (Apache-2.0). The layout idea follows Berlioz and Melliès, The latent space of equational theories (2026). Code, data and Lean files: github.com/anttiluode/Luotain.
